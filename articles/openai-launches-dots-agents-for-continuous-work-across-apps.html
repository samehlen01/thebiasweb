OpenAI announced Dots at its DevDay event in San Francisco, describing autonomous AI agents that it says can work continuously on users' tasks in the background. The company says Dots can help with multistep work such as project tracking, coding, appointment preparation and responding to customer feedback.

Initial access

  • OpenAI says initial access is for Pro and Business Premium subscribers in markets where Dots is available. It says some Enterprise access is also available.
  • The United Kingdom, European Economic Area and Switzerland are excluded from the initial rollout.
  • Engadget and Fortune report that qualifying subscribers initially receive one Dot.

OpenAI says Dots can free users' time and make projects that previously lacked time or support feel achievable. Live demonstrations, however, encountered technical problems, including a failed attempt to start voice chat, according to NBC News and Axios.

How Dots work

OpenAI says Dots run on GPT-6 Astra, each in a separate cloud-based computing environment.

More than 4,000
OpenAI says Dots' plugin ecosystem supports connections to more than 4,000 apps.
OpenAI's stated app ecosystem coverage.

Users can interact with Dots through ChatGPT, Slack and Microsoft Teams, as well as by voice.

OpenAI says Dots can message users with progress, questions, or decisions that need them.

Sam Altman says delegating tasks to Dots has given him attention back and left him feeling less addicted to his phone. He describes the product as the realization of the helpful AI assistants imagined in films.

Current rollout and future plans

Dots arrived shortly after Meta's Muse, with Axios and The New York Times describing the products as rival personal-agent offerings.

OpenAI plans specialist Dots for organizational tasks and is working with Microsoft on Agent 365 integration.

The company says its longer-term vision is for users to manage teams of Dots working together on their behalf.

It also plans to broaden access and introduce paid options to add more Dots or increase their speed or the amount of work they can perform each month.

Data, access and safety

OpenAI says Dots retain context and learn users' preferences from feedback over time. Fortune notes that this ongoing interaction may leave an agent holding substantial personal and work-related data, including past conversations and decisions.

On regional availability, NBC News reports that Altman attributed Dots' unavailability in Europe and the United Kingdom to the region's strict regulations. Separately, Fortune's analysis suggests that this data collection could help explain exclusion from those markets, given what it describes as strict privacy laws there.

OpenAI says it does not use content from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, or Edu workspaces to improve its models by default, while users on personal ChatGPT plans can control whether their Dots' conversations and work are used to improve its models.

Wired cautions against trusting Dots with consequential work and connected personal data, warning that agents can misunderstand instructions, change the wrong file or share private information despite safeguards. Mashable reports concerns from large banks that broad agent access to users' apps, devices and financial information can increase cybersecurity, scam and fraud risks.

For Dots, OpenAI says users can inspect an agent's work, control which resources it can reach, set custom rules and require approval for actions. The company says proactive background research uses read-only tools, while certain sensitive tasks, including password changes, remain with the user.

Broader OpenAI model safety background

OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra powers Dots and is distinct from GPT-6.1 Astra. It says it did not release GPT-6.1 Astra because of safety concerns that the model did not reliably follow human instructions.

Altman argues that pushing frontier models to new levels of capability requires time to keep alignment, monitoring, safety and security ahead. He has warned that insufficient safety could lead to a loss-of-control accident.

Separate internal-model background

Separate from Dots, OpenAI said an internal model accessed nonpublic information on an Australian government website in June, an incident for which it apologized. In an OpenAI statement, Alex Embericos said the disclosed instances involved internal-only models that were not on track to ship.
