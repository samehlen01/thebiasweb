On Saturday, Al Jazeera reported that thousands of people were detained in a sweeping security operation in Delhi.

Security restrictions around the protest site

Delhi police said they refused permission for the demonstration because the application had been submitted late. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) said it would proceed despite the police ban.

More than 25,000
Police and security personnel deployed across Delhi, according to The Guardian
The Guardian reported the Saturday deployment and said many personnel were brought in from other states.

The Guardian reported that authorities blocked access to Jantar Mantar and deployed more than 25,000 police and security personnel across Delhi. Al Jazeera reported that mobile internet services were suspended. Al Jazeera and The Guardian reported that the operation disrupted travel and access to central New Delhi.

Detentions and rights concerns

CJP leaders Abhijeet Dipke, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka were detained after arriving in Delhi by plane. The CJP said police deliberately intercepted its leaders before the peaceful protest began, arguing that the timing and targeting of the detentions showed an intent to disable the protest’s leadership rather than regulate the gathering.

Outlets reported detention figures in separate accounts without establishing that the counts covered identical periods, locations or categories of people. Associated Press reported that police detained more than 2,000 people, including opposition politicians, journalists and lawyers. Democracy Now reported that Scroll political editor Shoaib Daniyal was arrested on Saturday along with 7,000 other protesters. The Guardian reported that authorities detained tens of thousands of people during what it described as a lockdown.

Separately, The Guardian reported that YouTuber Dhruv Rathee was placed under house arrest while former Delhi chief minister Arvind Kejriwal was detained.

Democracy Now reported comments in which Jahnavi Sen accused police and paramilitary forces of unlawful, heavy-handed treatment of protesters and journalists. She said women had been put on buses without women constables and detained after dark, and that journalists were detained despite the Supreme Court’s direction against illegal detention.

The United Nations Human Rights Office expressed concern over mass detentions before and during the protests.

The United Nations Human Rights Office urged authorities to respect peaceful assembly. Amnesty International said the detentions signalled efforts to suppress critical voices. The Indian mission to the UN in Geneva called international concerns about the detentions “misplaced” and “an unwarranted interference in the internal affairs of India”. It said the right to peaceful assembly is guaranteed by India’s Constitution and is subject to reasonable restrictions in the interest of public order.

On Sunday, Al Jazeera reported that thousands of protesters were released after the Supreme Court called their detention “illegal”. Police officials said all detainees had been released that day.

The New York Times reported that scattered protests continued on Sunday after the crackdown before a large opposition demonstration on Monday. On Monday, thousands marched in Delhi, demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.

The Monday march was part of a campaign against the voter-roll revision. Opposition parties say the Special Intensive Revision removed more than 130 million names from voter rolls and disenfranchised their supporters. The Election Commission and the government reject allegations that the exercise was politically manipulated. Separately, the Supreme Court ordered Modi’s government and the Election Commission to respond to allegations surrounding the revision, according to The Guardian.

The dispute over voting rights

Protesters and opposition figures argue that the revision was designed to benefit Modi’s Bharatiya Janata Party by disenfranchising groups more likely to oppose it. They cite the scale of removals and allegations that Muslims and other minorities were disproportionately affected. Daniyal described the deletions as arbitrary and said the anger reflected a belief that people had lost their right to vote and their democracy. The Election Commission, government and BJP call the opposition’s allegations misinformation and say the exercise removes duplicate and ineligible entries. The BJP attributes the deletions to deaths, relocation and people who could not be traced. It credits its electoral performance to Modi’s popularity and economic growth.

The Guardian reported that the Indian Express had identified multiple irregularities in the revision process. Separately, a Guardian editorial reported that some election commissioners had raised concerns about voters’ names being removed without their knowledge.

With demonstrations continuing after the crackdown, NPR characterized the protests as a political challenge for Modi. The competing accounts of the voter-roll revision remained at the center of the protest campaign.
