Two pandas arrive in Atlanta under US-China loan agreement

Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, two giant pandas from China, arrived at Atlanta’s Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport on Sunday morning for a 10-year loan to Zoo Atlanta, the zoo said. The animals arrived under an agreement between the Chinese and US governments.

Chinese President Xi Jinping announced the planned arrival on Thursday during his three-day summit with US President Donald Trump, presenting the loan as a gesture of friendship. Associated Press reporting described it as one of the summit’s few concrete outcomes, contrasting the loan with the lack of fixes for long-standing differences between the two countries.

The zoo’s agreement had already been announced in April, without a specific arrival date. The pandas’ arrival is part of a conservation collaboration between Zoo Atlanta and the China Wildlife Conservation Association. The arrangement makes Atlanta one of three US zoos housing giant pandas, according to the zoo.

Friendship without major concessions

The summit’s agenda included artificial intelligence, tariffs, critical minerals, Taiwan and the Iran war, according to CNBC. In analysis reported by the outlet, Han Shen Lin argued that the panda gesture gives Beijing a way to send a visible message of friendship without having to make major policy concessions.

Associated Press reporting characterised China’s panda loans as diplomatic expressions of goodwill, noting that Beijing has allowed agreements to lapse when tensions arise. CNBC described the loans as a barometer of the US-China relationship, saying the programme had shifted from gifts to leases and had tracked the temperature of bilateral relations.

Analysts cited by CNBC expected the gesture to improve the atmosphere and create positive momentum in US-China relations. Their forecast concerned the tone of the relationship, rather than an agreement on the policy differences on the summit’s agenda.

For Zoo Atlanta’s president, Raymond B. King, the partnership’s most important benefit is conserving giant pandas. He said the collaboration enables important conservation work and helps guard the species. King also described the arrival as a thrilling and historic occasion for the zoo, Atlanta and Georgia.

From Chengdu to Atlanta

Both pandas were born in 2020 and had been kept at the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, according to Zoo Atlanta. Ping Ping is male and Fu Shuang is female, according to Xinhua News Agency.

Officials said the pair departed Chengdu early Sunday aboard a dedicated FedEx Panda Express Boeing 777. FedEx said both animals had been quarantined before leaving Chengdu and travelled in custom-built crates.

The pandas were the aircraft’s only cargo apart from their bamboo, water and treats, according to FedEx. The company said it donated the full cost of transporting the animals to support conservation efforts.

Before the arrival, Zoo Atlanta said it had upgraded the panda habitat, including adding more spacious day rooms. The zoo also secured locally grown Atlanta bamboo for the animals.

Visitors will have to wait to meet the new arrivals. Zoo Atlanta said Ping Ping and Fu Shuang are entering about a month of biosecure quarantine, and no date for public viewing has been confirmed.
