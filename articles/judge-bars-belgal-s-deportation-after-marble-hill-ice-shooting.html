Shooting, legal action and custody

A federal judge has temporarily barred Belgal’s deportation while his case proceeds. On October 8, an ICE officer shot Belgal during an immigration enforcement operation in Marble Hill. Belgal’s shooting and detention triggered legal action seeking his release and temporarily blocked his deportation. His lawyers separately filed an emergency habeas petition seeking his release and challenging the legal basis for his arrest and detention. His attorneys said immigration agents lacked a valid warrant for his arrest and that no notice to appear in immigration court had been authorized or issued before his arrest.

After hospital care, he was transferred to federal immigration custody at Brooklyn’s Metropolitan Detention Center. His lawyers said he remained in pain and had a bullet lodged in his body.

DHS said ICE was conducting a targeted operation to arrest a man from the Dominican Republic. Separately, federal officials alleged that Belgal refused orders to leave his vehicle, struck two ICE vehicles and accelerated toward officers. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the alleged escalation required force. The NYPD said its released video did not capture the shooting itself and did not show Belgal with a weapon; DHS did not describe him as armed. NYPD video showed an ICE officer in a construction vest carrying a long gun at the scene.

NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said at least one ICE officer fired seven rounds and Belgal was struck in the neck. His 5-year-old son was in the vehicle and was later found safe with a family member, she said. Mullin said agents did not know the child was in the car.

After the shooting, city officers responded to reports of gunfire, found Belgal handcuffed, rendered aid and took him to a hospital, Tisch said. She said the NYPD had not been involved in the federal operation before the shooting and that no city officers were present when shots were fired.

Community concerns and official responses

Pierina Sanchez and immigration advocates called ICE’s actions reckless, pointing to shots fired in a crowded area near schools while a child was in the car. Residents and local advocates said the shooting endangered and traumatized Belgal’s son. Documented and Gothamist reported that the shooting created fear, anger and disruption among Marble Hill residents, including anxiety among parents and children.

Residents and lawmakers said ICE’s presence around a nearby commercial parking lot had become pervasive and that they had warned for months it could turn violent. Target told Gothamist it had no agreement with ICE. Activist Hannah Stauss called the shooting a direct consequence of allowing ICE to use the lot as a staging area.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the shooting outrageous and urged ICE’s abolition. He said masked agents surrounded Belgal’s vehicle before firing, and argued that such raids terrorize immigrant residents and make New Yorkers less safe. The Intercept reported that legally there is limited ability for Mamdani to keep federal immigration agents out of the city.

DHS blamed sanctuary policies for forcing federal officers to make arrests in public rather than through local cooperation. The department argued that “when sanctuary politicians release dangerous undocumented people,” ICE must carry out arrests in “unpredictable and dangerous environments.”

The shooting prompted protests, confrontations with police and arrests in New York City. It also intensified demands that ICE halt or end enforcement operations in New York City. The NYPD said five people were arrested during Thursday-night protests that followed the shooting and charged with obstructing governmental administration and resisting arrest.

Police response and investigations

Immigration advocate Murad Awawdeh and local elected officials alleged that police helped ICE vehicles move through protesters. They argued that crowd control can enable federal operations even when city officers do not conduct immigration enforcement, and said such assistance amounted to improper collaboration.

The NYPD maintains that it does not conduct civil immigration enforcement. The Intercept reported that the department shares some information with federal agencies.

Mullin said ICE agents were wearing activated body cameras during the shooting. That footage is distinct from the NYPD video. Gov. Kathy Hochul called on federal authorities to provide a full and transparent account and release relevant footage. DHS has not publicly named the agent who fired the shots. Mullin said information would be released as the agency investigation moves forward, but TIME reported that ICE policy allows footage to be withheld under specific and compelling circumstances.

The Bronx district attorney’s investigation is separate from ICE’s internal agency investigation. Bronx District Attorney Darcel Clark said her office was investigating the shooting, while Mullin said ICE agents who discharged weapons would be placed on administrative leave during the agency’s investigation.
