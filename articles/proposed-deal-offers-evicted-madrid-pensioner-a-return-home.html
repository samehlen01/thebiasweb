Proposed deal offers evicted Madrid pensioner a return home

Maricarmen Abascal, an 87-year-old disabled tenant evicted from her Madrid home of more than 70 years, would be able to return under a proposed tenancy agreement at a rent similar to what she previously paid, her lawyer Beatriz Duro said.

The agreement remains unsigned and awaits Abascal’s approval. She did not participate in the meeting about the proposal, and the final rent figure remains unclear. Duro said both sides had shown goodwill.

The eviction triggered protests by tens of thousands of people and an encampment in Madrid’s Puerta del Sol. Duro credited social mobilisation for the proposed deal. RTÉ reported that the public outcry had increased pressure on Pedro Sánchez’s government to address housing policy.

Abascal was forcibly removed on a stretcher on 23 September. According to the Madrid renters’ union, courts had blocked three earlier attempts to evict her, but the Supreme Court ultimately ruled in favour of the property firm.

Urbagestión, which bought the property in 2018, raised the stated monthly rent from €500 ($568.67 / £429.04) to €2,650 ($3,014 / £2,274). It later offered €1,650 ($1,877 / £1,416) — still more than Abascal’s monthly pension of €1,350 ($1,535 / £1,158), leaving the reduced offer beyond her means.

Abascal called the property company and the authorities responsible for her eviction inhumane. She accused the company of acting to make money and questioned whether Sánchez recognised the daily scale of evictions. Duro described the force used to remove her as disproportionate and unnecessary.

Amnesty International Spain called the eviction a human-rights violation, arguing that a speculative fund’s interests had been put ahead of Abascal’s right to housing. It said the case reflected the suffering of thousands of households and demanded bold institutional action rather than expressions of regret.

Isabel Díaz Ayuso emphasised that the eviction was court-authorised. She described Abascal’s case as one among many such evictions in Spain, citing an annual total of 25,500.

Housing costs and political choices

The dispute comes amid a shortage of homes and rising costs. The BBC reported a Bank of Spain estimate that the country is short of 700,000 homes, based on the gap between demand and new construction. Other reporting cited the bank’s estimate of about 550,000 homes last year.

INE figures for the second quarter of 2026 showed house prices up 12.2% from a year earlier. Rental costs rose by 8.5% in 2025, according to property firm Idealista. Subsidised public rental housing represents less than 2% of Spain’s housing stock, NPR reported, citing OECD comparisons.

Sánchez described Abascal’s eviction as a social tragedy and framed housing as a matter of humanity that transcends politics. He said his government would bring forward a housing Royal Decree and relief measures.

His Socialist-led governing coalition lacks a parliamentary majority. RTÉ reported that the prospects for a new housing initiative were unclear, noting that a similar housing bill had been defeated earlier in the year.

A six-year moratorium protecting some vulnerable tenants from eviction was ended earlier in the year by votes from the People’s party, Vox and Junts. Spain also passed tenant-protection and rent-control legislation in 2023, but the Guardian reported that People’s party-led regions had avoided implementing parts of it.

Housing Minister Isabel Rodríguez accused Madrid’s conservative regional government of favouring property speculators. She said the national minority government had repeatedly tried to enact a ban on evictions such as Abascal’s.

Ayuso accused Sánchez’s government of responding too late to the housing shortage and proposing sectarian, ideological policies. She argued that Spain needs one million additional homes to accommodate demographic growth, which she attributed to policies encouraged by his government.

Unai Sordo called for investment funds to be excluded from the housing market and for tax rules that make hoarding homes unprofitable. He argued that homes should serve residents rather than large corporations.

The Madrid Tenants’ Union is demanding indefinite rental contracts and an end to evictions like Abascal’s. Carme Arcarazo has called for a rent freeze and anti-eviction measures, rejecting temporary fixes in favour of durable guarantees of decent housing.

The union says its supporters will remain camped in Puerta del Sol until new laws are passed.
