ASOS said an unauthorised customer notification was sent through its app at about 10am. It said it immediately restricted access to its notification platforms and was investigating unauthorised activity involving third-party platforms it uses to communicate with customers.

ASOS said basic personal information, including names and contact details, may have been accessed. It said it did not believe payment-card information or account passwords had been affected.

What is known about the unverified access claim

The claimed Snowflake compromise remains unverified, and the scope and route of any wider access remain unestablished. The notification, attributed to the Xuanye Group, claimed that its sender had fully compromised an ASOS Snowflake instance and threatened to leak data unless the company engaged with it. It included a link to a Telegram chat.

Snowflake said its investigation, launched following the notification, had found no compromise of its platform; that finding did not establish whether ASOS's specific Snowflake environment or customer data had been accessed. The Register noted that the notification alone did not establish that its sender had accessed ASOS's Snowflake instance or sensitive customer data.

What customers should do now

Dan Bird advised customers not to click the notification's Telegram link and to be wary of follow-up messages claiming to come from ASOS. Marijus Briedis warned that the incident could create conditions for phishing attempts impersonating ASOS, including emails or texts about passwords, payments, orders or refunds.

ASOS response and business impact

ASOS said it was working with internal and external specialist advisers. The Lancashire Post, Shropshire Star and The Guardian reported that the National Cyber Security Centre had offered assistance to the retailer.

More than 10%
Reported ASOS share-price fall
Reports said ASOS shares fell by more than 10% after news of the notification, with the initial decline put between about 11% and more than 14%; the timing does not establish that the notification caused the move.

ASOS said it was too early to quantify any potential effect on trading. It also said it had cyber-security insurance with a large global provider, including business-continuity insurance. ASOS said its website and app were operating normally, with no current disruption to its operations.

Possible explanations and the trusted channel

Snowflake is a platform businesses use to store large volumes of data. Jake Moore said that, if attackers had genuinely compromised an ASOS Snowflake environment, a large amount of customer information could be at risk.

One possible explanation, suggested by Rob Demain, is that any compromise might be confined to customer-engagement or marketing systems. He said access at that level could account for the app notification without proving access to wider retail infrastructure or data theft. Dan Bird outlined a distinct conditional scenario. He said that, if both the alleged Snowflake compromise and app-notification access were genuine, attackers might have obtained credentials allowing access to more than one system. He said sending the push notification would require access to a notification system separate from the claimed Snowflake data platform.

Using ASOS's own customer-notification channel for an extortion message was unusually brazen and serious, Charlotte Wilson said. Customers normally expect app notifications to come directly from the company; in this case, the trusted channel carried a public demand for engagement and a threat of disclosure. Marie Wilcox argued that broadcasting the claim to users was a form of psychological pressure. In her assessment, the aim was to generate panic and push ASOS towards considering a quick payment rather than responding rationally to the threat.

The group's Telegram channel had only been created on the day the notification was sent, according to reporting by the BBC and The Guardian. Aiden Sinnott suggested that the notification might have been intended to attract wider attention for the newly announced group.
