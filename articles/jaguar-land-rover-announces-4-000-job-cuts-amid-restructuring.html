JLR plans to reduce its global workforce by around 4,000 roles over the next two years. It has opened a voluntary redundancy programme for salaried and management staff. JLR employs 44,000 people globally. Although it has confirmed a global reduction, it has not published a detailed site-by-site distribution. The Guardian reported that the cuts are expected to fall chiefly on UK office, salaried and management roles rather than production workers.

JLR said compulsory redundancies on less generous terms could follow if too few people choose to leave voluntarily.

Financial pressure and investment plans

JLR reported revenue of £6 billion ($8.11 billion) for the three months to 30 June, down 9.6% year on year. Car volumes fell 9.2%. JLR is targeting £1.7 billion ($2.3 billion) of savings over two years. It said the savings plan would lower its break-even point, reduce complexity and allow it to cover costs at lower sales volumes while competing in a rapidly changing market.

Balaji said JLR needs to reduce organisational complexity, improve efficiency and build resilience as it faces technological change, intense competition and geopolitical uncertainty. He said the changes are needed to strengthen competitiveness and secure its long-term future.

JLR says the measures will support continued investment of £15 billion ($20.3 billion) to £18 billion ($24.3 billion) over five years in electrification, digital technology, advanced manufacturing and customer experience; it plans to launch five products within 12 months. Balaji forecasts double-digit revenue growth, which he links to the launches, JLR's brands and renewed attention to North America and other markets.

Separately, a cyberattack forced JLR to stop production at its UK factories for five weeks.

Industrial policy and response

Sharon Graham of Unite blamed years of underinvestment and high industrial energy costs for pressure on the automotive sector. She also called the zero-emission vehicle mandate unsustainable and said further action was needed.

The UK Sustainable Investment and Finance Association said the mandate is essential because it gives investors a dependable, predictable path for growth in the electric-vehicle market and helps attract finance to the transition.

Professor David Bailey said high UK energy costs act as a competitiveness tax on British industry because electricity is a basic manufacturing input that can make British-built cars structurally more expensive.

The Guardian reported that Unite officials are expected to press JLR to prioritise retraining, redeployment or voluntary redundancy over compulsory redundancies if the scheme does not generate enough departures. Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds ruled out a bailout for JLR but plans talks with the company and Unite.

West Midlands support and site uncertainty

£500,000
Initial West Midlands employment-support package
($675,930) Richard Parker announced the funding for JLR workers accepting voluntary redundancy.

West Midlands Mayor Richard Parker said the package would help voluntary leavers find new jobs, connect with employers and obtain skills and careers support. He said it was intended to keep advanced-manufacturing and automotive skills connected to opportunities in the regional economy.

Parker warned that the redundancies could affect workers, their families, communities and the supply chain.

Liam Byrne described the cuts as a body blow to workers, families and West Midlands communities and called for urgent help to enable affected people to find work. He said the need for support did not disappear because departures were voluntary.

Parker's package may be expanded with Department for Work and Pensions backing, but additional funding has not been confirmed.

The Liverpool Echo reported that Halewood is likely to be less severely affected than other JLR sites because it is principally a production plant and the cuts are reported to focus on salaried and management roles. No site-by-site allocation has been confirmed.

State of play

  • Whether voluntary departures will be sufficient to avoid compulsory redundancies on less generous terms.
  • How the global reduction will be distributed across sites.
  • Whether the £500,000 ($675,930) package may be expanded with Department for Work and Pensions backing.
  • What follows from Jonathan Reynolds's planned talks with JLR and Unite.
