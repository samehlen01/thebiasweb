Shouyong Zhang and Shili Chen, both 77, are the parents of Candice Jang, Jonathan McKinsey’s estranged wife, and McKinsey’s parents-in-law. They have been arrested and charged with murder in his killing.

McKinsey, 40, was a New York Times Games engineering executive who had worked for the company since 2023. He was shot multiple times in a parking lot at Dublin Sports Grounds in Dublin, California, on Saturday and pronounced dead there, police said.

Charges and evidence

Alameda County prosecutors allege that the married couple took turns firing at McKinsey. Surveillance footage, prosecutors say, supports their allegation that both were shooters. Police said three witnesses identified the couple as shooters. The murder charges include allegations that Zhang and Chen lay in wait to kill him, characterizing the shooting as premeditated. According to prosecutors, Zhang admitted shooting McKinsey and firearms connected to the alleged crime were recovered.

The contested family-law record

McKinsey and his estranged wife, Candice Jang, were involved in divorce and child-custody litigation, with a hearing scheduled shortly after the killing. The claims described in the parties’ filings were competing, unadjudicated allegations; the temporary orders did not resolve their merits.

Jang alleged that McKinsey had abused her and their children and that the children were unsafe in his unsupervised care. In seeking a protection order, she cited alleged physical incidents involving the children and what she described as a long history of physical, verbal and emotional abuse. A judge granted Jang a temporary domestic-violence restraining order against McKinsey.

McKinsey separately sought protection against Jang. Court records show that a temporary order on that request was granted in part. In court filings, he alleged that Jang had abused him for more than 15 years, including beating him with a frying pan, and that her parents, including Chen, had abused or demeaned him and his child. His filings also said Chen used anti-LGBTQ slurs toward him and one of the children.

McKinsey had been charged with misdemeanor child-abuse-related offenses and pleaded not guilty. He was seeking a mental-health diversion program. The Daily Mail, citing Mercury News reporting, said completing it would have resulted in his child-related charges being dropped.

McKinsey’s lawyer, Andrea Yah, described his death as a tragedy and said those responsible should be held accountable. She criticized media coverage as one-sided and sensational, arguing that reporting focused solely on allegations against him did not reflect the complicated family-law record. The motive for the shooting remains unresolved. Dublin Police Captain Michael Campos said investigators were broadly reviewing the histories and relationships of people connected to McKinsey and the suspects, including abuse allegations and disputes involving gender identity; those matters have not been identified as a motive.
