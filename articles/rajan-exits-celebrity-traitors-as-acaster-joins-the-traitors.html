Maya Jama and Richard E Grant were selected as traitors in The Celebrity Traitors’ series-two opener.

James Acaster was recruited as the third traitor. Rajan expected Acaster’s recruitment to divert suspicion from Grant and help protect the existing traitors. Rajan was murdered in the game at the start of the second episode.

The 21 celebrity contestants are playing at Ardross Castle in the Scottish Highlands for up to £100,000 ($132,220) for their chosen charities. Their first mission, at Loch Glass, added £15,000 ($19,833) to the charity prize pot.

8.9 million
Average audience for the opening episode
Reported by the Shropshire Star: the opening episode was described as the most-watched debut episode of any series in the Traitors franchise in the UK; it averaged 8.9 million viewers and peaked at 9.1 million.

Acaster viewed Jama as not suspected by the other contestants and Grant as a loose cannon. He said he needed to work out how to survive while helping to protect Grant.

Rajan reflects on his exit

Rajan said he had played the wrong game. With hindsight, he felt he had become the journalist asking awkward questions, despite intending to stay quiet to avoid murder or banishment. Rajan’s advice to players was to stay under the radar and say little in the opening episodes.

Rajan nevertheless believed he could have made an effective traitor if recruited. He reasoned that having genuinely spent a day as a Faithful had helped him understand that role and would have better equipped him to perform as a traitor.

After the graveyard mission, Hall’s use of the code word “haggis” restricted eligibility for that night’s murder to Hall and Hannah Fry.

Grant’s declaration divides the castle

Grant regarded his public declaration that he was a traitor as a serious strategic error, calling it possibly the biggest mistake he had ever made. He said he needed to reduce the attention it had brought him.

Julie Hesmondhalgh argued that Grant should be banished, saying that after Grant declared he was a traitor, it was irrational to target somebody else on no evidence. Romesh Ranganathan took a similar position, saying Grant’s declaration had forced the group’s hand.

Other contestants questioned whether Grant would openly declare himself a traitor if he were a real traitor. Some interpreted his declaration as a calculated double bluff or eccentric acting rather than treating it as genuine.

I just don't believe Richard's a traitor, I've known him 30 years and he's my neighbour.
— Jerry Hall

The second episode ended before the first round-table banishment vote was revealed. It remained unclear whether Grant’s declaration had persuaded any contestants that it was a double bluff.
