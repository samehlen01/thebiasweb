The event, Five arrested over suspected terrorist plot near RAF Fairford, concerns five men detained after police received a report of vehicles apparently heading toward the air base. It prompted a counter-terrorism operation, evacuations near the base and competing accounts of whether authorities had advance knowledge of a potential plot.

12:45 a.m.
Time police received the suspicious-vehicle report
27 September, according to Metropolitan Police Counter Terrorism Policing

Counter Terrorism Policing said the caller reported three suspicious vehicles that appeared to be travelling towards RAF Fairford. Armed officers then arrested five men in the Whelford area. The initial public account did not establish a target, the precise nature of any intended plan or a motive for the suspected activity.

5
Men arrested in the Whelford area
Police later said all five remained in custody

The men were first arrested on suspicion of offences under the Explosives Act, according to the police account. They were subsequently arrested on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act under section 5 of the Terrorism Act. Those suspicions explain the use of counter-terrorism powers, but the available official statements did not set out evidence proving a completed attack or publicly describe the alleged plan.

Vehicles examined as residents evacuated

400 metres
Cordon imposed around vehicles near RAF Fairford
Army explosive ordnance personnel examined the vehicles

Police established the cordon while Army explosive ordnance disposal specialists examined the vehicles. The measure was a precaution during an unfolding inquiry rather than a public confirmation that explosives had been found. Gloucestershire Police and other local officials said they believed the incident was contained, although the vehicle examinations and wider investigation were still continuing.

85
Households evacuated as a precaution
Evacuations took place during the explosive-ordnance examination

The disruption extended beyond the police cordon. Some evacuated residents could not return home on Sunday night, and Gloucestershire Police and Cotswold District Council arranged overnight accommodation for people unable to stay with relatives or friends. That practical response reflects the uncertainty facing residents while officers continued to assess the vehicles and any potential risk.

Vicki Evans, speaking for Counter Terrorism Policing, described the case as still developing and confirmed that the five men remained detained. Her statement underscored that the investigation had not reached conclusions about motive, target selection or the scope of any alleged preparation.

We are in the early stages of an investigation, and five men remain in custody,
— Vicki Evans

The US Air Force's 501st Combat Support Wing said no US Air Force personnel were involved in the incident. It said US and UK military authorities would remain vigilant and take appropriate measures to protect service members, civilian staff, contractors, families and local communities. Gloucestershire Police and US military partners also expressed the view that the security response was capable of containing the incident and safeguarding those affected.

Separate security-alert reports require a geographic distinction. RAF Lakenheath confirmed that it was at the US Department of Defense's Charlie alert level, while reports said RAF Fairford had been raised to Delta, a status that was not independently confirmed. These accounts concern different installations and different levels of confirmation, so they do not amount to competing descriptions of the alert at one base.

Dispute over prior intelligence

The sharpest factual disagreement concerns whether the operation followed prior intelligence. The Press Association, the BBC and the Guardian reported that the operation was not intelligence-led and that investigators had not known in advance of a possible plot. Donald Trump, however, said the suspects had been under investigation or surveillance for a long time. Those versions materially differ over whether authorities had prior awareness of the alleged threat.

Trump also praised the UK-US cooperation behind the arrests, characterising it as an amazing and successful operation. His account presented the detentions as the culmination of an established investigation rather than an improvised response to a public report.

We had them under view for a long time, and we got them.
— Donald Trump

Trump further alleged that the men had been seeking to cause major damage at RAF Fairford or to US forces there. That allegation has not been publicly established by counter-terrorism police, which had not set out an intended target, a detailed plan or a confirmed motive. The difference matters because Trump's description goes beyond the information released in the police account of arrests on suspicion.

Motive remains an unresolved question

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting urged the public not to speculate while investigators establish the facts. His position was that the inquiry was at an early stage, the underlying motive had not been determined and it would be irresponsible to draw conclusions before the evidence was known. That call for restraint directly contrasts with political and editorial efforts to connect the incident to wider international tensions.

Investigators were considering whether Iran could have had a role in the suspected plot, but no Iranian connection had been confirmed. This was reported as a line of inquiry, not a conclusion. Authorities were also said to be considering other possible explanations, including a jihadist motive, a Russian link or another motive entirely, while they analysed seized devices and other evidence.

The Mirror editorial board argued that the arrests illustrated how the US-Iran war and Britain's support for US operations could bring security risks home to Britain. The editorial based that analysis on Britain's permission for US bombers to operate from its territory and on Iran's earlier warning that bases used to attack it could be targets. The Guardian separately assessed that a proven Iranian connection would substantially escalate hostility between the UK and Iran. Both are interpretations or projected consequences, not confirmed findings about this case.

One further unverified account adds to the disagreement over how the operation began. An unnamed Whelford resident's account suggested that a chance 999 call may have disrupted or foiled whatever the men had been planning. That possibility is speculative: it rests on reporting about suspicious vans and masked men, rather than a confirmed police finding that the call prevented an attack.

State of play

What is established across the accounts is that police acted on the suspicious-vehicle report, arrested five men, imposed a cordon, evacuated nearby households and continued their investigation while the men remained in custody. Officials said the incident appeared contained, but vehicle examinations and evidence-gathering were still under way. Prior intelligence remains disputed between the reporting cited by the Press Association, BBC and Guardian and Trump's account, while the alleged scale of any plan and any Iranian, jihadist, Russian or other motive remain unconfirmed. The next publicly indicated steps are the completion of the vehicle examinations and the continuing counter-terrorism investigation.
