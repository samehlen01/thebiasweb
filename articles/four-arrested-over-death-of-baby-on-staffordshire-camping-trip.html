Four arrested over death of baby on Staffordshire camping trip is the event at the centre of a Staffordshire Police investigation after a three-month-old girl died on Saturday. Police said emergency services were called to woodland off Strawberry Lane near Great Wyrley, before four people were arrested on suspicion of murder.

West Midlands Ambulance Service said its response included two ambulances, two paramedic officers and a helicopter operated by Midlands Air Ambulance Charity. Staffordshire Police said the child had been found in cardiac arrest, while the ambulance service said crews began advanced life support immediately but were unable to save her. The police account placed the alarm on Saturday morning.

From emergency call to murder arrests

Staffordshire Police said a 42-year-old man and a 30-year-old woman, both from South Staffordshire, were among those arrested. The other two people were a 20-year-old woman from Wolverhampton and an 18-year-old man from Walsall. Police said all four arrests were made at the scene and that the four were later released on conditional bail as inquiries continued.

Police had initially described the death as unexplained and said the inquiry was at an early stage before the murder arrests were announced. Detective Inspector Josh Lee later said investigators were still gathering evidence and keeping an open mind about the precise circumstances. The two statements describe the inquiry at different points: the earlier unexplained-death assessment preceded the arrests, while police still said the cause and circumstances had not been determined.

Location and police presence

Reporting on the investigation also described a police cordon around a fishing lake in the Wyrley and Essington canal nature reserve. That is a more detailed description of the area under inquiry than the initial police account of woodland off Strawberry Lane near Great Wyrley, rather than a report of a separate incident location. Officers were examining the area while making inquiries.

The event has been described as involving a camping trip, but local resident Malcolm Ward offered a narrower description of the land itself. Ward said it was not a campsite, characterising it instead as woodland used by people walking dogs and fishing. His account is a local view of how the place is used, rather than a police finding about the death or the reason for the emergency response.

Police said officers would remain locally both to pursue inquiries and to reassure residents. Lee said the incident had understandably worried the community and that detectives were working around the clock. He also urged people not to circulate unconfirmed claims online, saying the public should instead use factual police updates while the investigation continues.

The ambulance service’s account establishes the resources sent to the scene and its efforts to resuscitate the baby, but it does not resolve why she was in cardiac arrest. That unanswered question is consistent with the police position that the precise circumstances remain undetermined while evidence is being obtained. Earlier, Detective Inspector Katie Gregory had likewise said the death was being treated as unexplained.

Calls for restraint and respect

Lee’s warning about online claims was echoed by Robert Duncan, the Cheslyn Hay district councillor. Duncan said residents should give emergency workers room to carry out their duties with dignity, avoid speculation and respect the baby’s family. His request reflected his view that full confirmed details had not formally been shared with him at that stage.

Police have also said they are treating the incident as isolated, an assessment Lee made while stressing that the investigation remains at an early stage. That assessment is explicitly provisional: investigators say they are still collecting evidence and have not fixed the precise circumstances of the child’s death. The request for residents to avoid unsupported online accounts is therefore tied directly to the unresolved nature of the inquiry.

State of play

What is confirmed in the police and ambulance accounts is that a three-month-old girl died after emergency services attended woodland near Great Wyrley, and that four people were arrested on suspicion of murder before being released on conditional bail. Police have alleged no further account of how the death occurred: they say its circumstances remain undetermined, though they are treating the incident as isolated. Officers are expected to remain in the area as inquiries and community reassurance continue.
