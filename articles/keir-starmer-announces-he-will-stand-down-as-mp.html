Former prime minister Keir Starmer has announced that he will stand down as MP for Holborn and St Pancras, which he has represented since 2015. His departure is expected to trigger a by-election once the required parliamentary process is completed.

After reflecting over the summer, Starmer said it was the right time to hand the seat to another Labour representative. The decision reverses his previously reported plan to remain an MP until the next general election. Starmer said he would support Labour’s selected candidate and continue supporting the government of Andy Burnham, the new prime minister.

Procedure and expected by-election

To formalise his departure, Starmer must take one of two unpaid Crown offices: Steward and Bailiff of the Manor of Northstead or Steward and Bailiff of the Chiltern Hundreds. A Treasury spokesperson said an announcement of which office Starmer would receive was expected on September 1, 2026, but no appointment had been confirmed. Until that appointment is made, the by-election is expected rather than formally initiated. After the appointment, a by-election will elect a new MP to replace him.

Once the Crown appointment has been made, Labour’s chief whip must move the writ to begin the by-election process. The vote must then take place within the statutory timetable. While the writ and polling date remain unconfirmed, Labour has scheduled candidate-selection dates.

  1. After the Crown appointment, Labour’s chief whip must move the writ, beginning the by-election process.
  2. Labour’s candidate-selection applications are scheduled to close at noon on September 4, 2026.
  3. Hustings and a selection meeting are planned for September 9, 2026.

The by-election is expected in 2026, but its polling date remains unconfirmed. October 2026 has been reported as a possibility.

Electoral context

Holborn and St Pancras was created in 1983 and has only ever been held by Labour. Starmer won the seat by more than 11,000 votes at the 2024 general election, below the 27,763-vote margin he secured in 2019.

In the 2026 Camden council elections, Labour retained control with 30 of 55 seats, down from 47 in 2022, while the Greens won 11. Seven of the Green seats were in the part of the borough covered by Holborn and St Pancras. Council results do not directly predict a Westminster result, though the Daily Mail’s analysis suggested the Green gains could offer an opening for a parliamentary breakthrough.

Campaign activity and possible candidates

The Green Party has said it will deploy activists and poster boards in the constituency. Hollinrake said the Conservatives would campaign for a stronger economy and a stronger country, while arguing that Labour was preparing tax rises. He said the by-election would impose a cost on taxpayers.

Andrew Feinstein came second in the 2024 general election with 7,312 votes. He has argued that progressive forces should unite behind a representative who would work for local people. The Evening Standard’s analysis suggested that some independent and Green support may have come from former Labour voters protesting Starmer’s position on Gaza.

Green leader Zack Polanski has described the by-election as a referendum on ending “rip-off Britain”, framing that as the party’s argument for change. Polanski may stand himself, but has not formally declared a candidacy.

Count Binface, the electoral persona of comedian and writer Jon Harvey, has stood eight times since 2017. His online response to the expected vacancy was interpreted as a hint that he may run, not a formal declaration.

Robert Ford said he believed the contest could be highly competitive, citing what he described as recent movement from Labour to the Greens among younger voters, graduates and non-white residents.

In Labour’s candidate-selection debate, former Hampstead and Highgate MP Tulip Siddiq objected to prospective candidates who had not campaigned locally during the Camden elections. Without backing a contender, she argued that Starmer’s successor should have demonstrable local roots.

Starmer’s plans and political reaction

I will now focus on other things and that will be international affairs including defence, security, trade and technology in a fast-changing world.
— Keir Starmer

Starmer said he would also continue working with others to combat violence against women and girls. He described representing Holborn and St Pancras as an honour and privilege, and said he remained proud of his local work on affordable housing, education and efforts with Camden’s young people to address knife crime.

Burnham praised Starmer’s public service, citing his work for people seeking justice, his role on Ukraine and his support for the Hillsborough Law, while crediting him with leading Labour from defeat to electoral victory. Nick Thomas-Symonds said Starmer’s premiership left the country in better condition and that he transformed Labour from its worst defeat since 1935 into a 2024 landslide winner. The Washington Post characterised the departure as the end of Starmer’s domestic political career.

The expected by-election is an early political test for Burnham and Labour. The Independent’s analysis suggested that Starmer’s departure may ease concerns in Burnham’s team about a pro-Starmer faction undermining the new prime minister. Conservative frontbencher Kevin Hollinrake called the resignation another U-turn.

Dominic Ford called the departure sensible, saying it would have been difficult for Starmer to sit behind the new prime minister and describing him as an honest, effective local MP. Lewis Galloway, by contrast, called the exit premature and regrettable, saying he valued former prime ministers Theresa May and Rishi Sunak continuing constituency work from the backbenches.

An unnamed Kentish Town constituent blamed Starmer for damaging the country and harming pensioners. Laura Hyland said she welcomed his exit because she believed he had offered too little support for Palestine, and criticised arrests of people carrying signs backing Palestine Action. Annie said residents had had enough of Starmer, while Anita judged him wholly ineffective.
