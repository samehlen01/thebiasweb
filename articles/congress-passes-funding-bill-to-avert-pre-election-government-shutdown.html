Congress Passes Funding Bill to Avert Pre-Election Government Shutdown

After the Senate approved the continuing resolution, it was sent to President Donald Trump for signature. The House then passed the temporary measure 370-48. The resolution would keep the government funded through December 11, averting an immediate pre-election shutdown. Existing federal funding was due to expire September 30.

370-48
House vote on the continuing resolution
The measure passed with bipartisan support.

Nineteen Republicans and 29 Democrats voted against the measure. House leaders used the suspension-of-the-rules procedure, which required a two-thirds majority and Democratic votes for passage.

ABC News noted that the House acted weeks before October 1, when a lapse would have begun after funding expired September 30, while members were campaigning for reelection. The vote postponed the broader funding dispute until after the midterms. NPR, The Guardian and The New York Times interpreted the move as an effort to defuse the spending dispute, a strategy that postponed a more difficult fight until after the midterms.

What the stopgap measure changes

  • Delays until December 11 a White House Office of Management and Budget rule concerning political appointees’ authority to halt federal grants.
  • Bars the Department of Homeland Security from shifting funds to the Border Patrol.

Apart from those exceptions, the resolution generally keeps federal agencies funded largely at existing levels through December 11.

December 11
Scheduled expiration of temporary federal funding
Agencies are expected to remain funded largely at current levels until then.

Representative Tom Cole said the resolution would provide certainty that the government would remain open and that service members would be paid. Speaker Mike Johnson said it would avert the prospect of troops being deployed without paychecks.

Representative Rosa DeLauro called the delay to the grant rule an important first step but said more action was needed to stop the policy. She said disaster assistance should not depend on how a community voted. House Democrats said the rule could, if allowed to take effect, permit the Trump administration to cancel grants broadly and steer funding away from Democratic-led states.

Why leaders chose a stopgap

NPR said congressional leaders turned to the short-term bill after bipartisan Senate negotiations on full-year appropriations broke down.

Johnson cast passage as responsible Republican leadership, saying House Republicans had acted responsibly and started the process early enough to prevent a shutdown; he also called any possible shutdown a Democratic shutdown.

Representative Chip Roy opposed extending existing funding policies, arguing that the resolution would continue financing policies Congress had spent months trying to change. Freedom Caucus chair Andy Harris said he would vote to block the rest of the Republican agenda for the week in objection to the spending measure.

The spending fight ahead

Congress must negotiate full-year appropriations before temporary funding expires December 11.

  • House Republicans want future spending legislation to add hundreds of billions of dollars to military spending while reducing non-defense programmes.
  • House Democrats insist on a bipartisan framework that maintains equal domestic and military funding.

Senator Patty Murray said the next spending bills should invest in families, improve affordability and restrain what she described as President Trump’s war budget and corrupt administrative abuses.

The Independent forecast that reaching a full-year agreement would be difficult because the parties’ military and domestic-spending priorities conflict. The delayed Office of Management and Budget grant rule is also set to return on December 11.
