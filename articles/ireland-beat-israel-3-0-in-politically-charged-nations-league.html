The Republic of Ireland beat Israel 3-0 in the UEFA Nations League in Debrecen, Hungary, a neutral venue, securing its first victory of the campaign. Troy Parrott, Adam Idah and Jack Moylan scored Ireland's three goals before half-time. The match was played before a limited attendance and came amid calls for Ireland to boycott the fixture over the Israel-Gaza conflict.

Israel coach Ran Ben-Shimon said his side had failed mentally and tactically under pressure. He said Israel would need to find the energy to recover for its next game.

Players' decision and gestures

Ireland's players wore black armbands, bowed their heads during the Israeli anthem and did not take part in pre-match handshakes with Israel's players. Football Association of Ireland (FAI) chief executive David Courell said the armbands were worn "in recognition of all lives lost in the conflict."

Courell said a significant majority of the Ireland squad voted to proceed with the fixture. He said the vote followed four hours of conversations.

Goalkeeper Gavin Bazunu withdrew from the Ireland squad and did not play against Israel, saying he believed participation would be wrong because of his convictions about the killing of innocent people. He said his opposition was to atrocities in the region, not to Israeli people or Jewish communities. For UEFA registration purposes following Bazunu's withdrawal, defender Jimmy Dunne was listed as Ireland's third goalkeeper. Former Ireland manager Brian Kerr described Bazunu's decision as heroic.

Ireland manager Heimir Hallgrímsson called the days before the match the most difficult of his coaching career, but praised his players for representing themselves with dignity. He said their actions had brought worldwide attention to their position, which he said is normally the point of a protest.

Boycott calls and potential consequences

More than 160
Irish athletes signed an open letter
The letter urged the FAI to reconsider playing Israel.

Irish Sport for Palestine urged the FAI to reconsider the fixtures. Several supporters interviewed before the match also said Ireland should not play Israel: Jack Scurry opposed Israel's participation in the tournament; Brian O'Neill argued that Ireland's players should refuse to facilitate the game; and Elaine Fitzgerald said Ireland should stand with Palestinians because of its own history.

Fitzgerald also said the FAI should not have been involved in the squad vote and that the players could have shown greater solidarity with Bazunu.

Irish soccer officials forecast losses of at least $6.3 million (£4.76 million) and possible UEFA sanctions if the fixtures were boycotted, while FAI delegates feared significant and lasting harm to the association. Taoiseach Micheál Martin argued that an Irish boycott undertaken alone could be self-defeating, harming Irish football without changing Israel's position. Hallgrímsson said the consequences of refusing to play were unknown, that there was no course every person would accept, and that the group had to try to limit the damage.

The wider political argument

Hallgrímsson said, "Ireland were not playing with genocide but against it." The Israel Football Association accused him of "ignorance, hypocrisy and stupidity."

Beyond the football dispute, Israeli authorities rejected findings by a UN commission that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza.

Martin said Israel's military response in Gaza had been absolutely disproportionate. He rejected the suggestion that Ireland tolerates antisemitism, saying the country has no tolerance for it. He supported Israel's security and right to exist, alongside Palestinians' right to live peacefully in their homeland. He also said Ireland held a strong pro-Palestinian position and argued that more humanitarian aid should reach Gaza.

Next Israel fixture

Ireland were scheduled to play Israel behind closed doors in Backa Topola, Serbia. Hallgrímsson said he hoped the players would not need to repeat their pre-match protest gestures at the next fixture and that the public would understand their stance.
