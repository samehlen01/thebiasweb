A federal judge ordered Wilber Rafael Garces Perez to remain in federal pretrial custody on a federal assault charge rather than be returned to the South Texas ICE facility. U.S. Magistrate Judge Dustin M. Howell found him a flight risk but rejected prosecutors' contention that he posed a danger to the community.

Garces Perez, 28, was shot by an ICE officer in Austin on September 20 while making DoorDash deliveries. He faces one count of assaulting, resisting and impeding a federal officer. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Western District of Texas said he could face up to 20 years in prison if convicted.

Disputed encounter and video evidence

The federal complaint alleges that Garces Perez abruptly closed his window and drove away, striking an officer in the torso with his driver's-side mirror. Garces Perez, however, told investigators that a law-enforcement SUV struck his car, causing it to spin, before an officer fired and shot him in the back.

Body-camera footage shown in court captured the initial encounter but did not record the shooting or conclusively establish whether the alleged mirror contact occurred. Defense lawyers said the footage was insufficient because it did not fully record the moment Garces Perez drove away and the investigator could not confirm that the mirror struck the officer. According to Homeland Security Investigations Special Agent Rick Bazan, the officer who fired was not wearing a body camera. Bazan said the other officer's camera was off during or before the pursuit. Garces Perez's legal team criticized agents for conducting high-risk stops without wearing or activating accountability cameras.

According to the affidavit, agents confirmed that an immigration judge had issued a final order of removal against Garces Perez. Separately, Bazan testified about ICE's broader enforcement practices in Austin, according to KUT News. He said officers run license plates at locations across the city to identify people with immigration enforcement orders.

Investigations and calls for scrutiny

The Travis County district attorney's office requested evidence from federal authorities, saying the shooting might have involved violations of Texas law.

Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis said Austin police, the Texas Rangers and the Department of Homeland Security were expected to investigate. In a separate statement, DHS said Homeland Security Investigations would investigate with FBI support.

Austin Mayor Kirk Watson called for an independent investigation, arguing that a properly conducted inquiry would strengthen credibility and allow residents to learn what happened. U.S. Representative Greg Casar also backed outside scrutiny.

Medical care in dispute

Attorney Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch said Garces Perez was discharged after roughly four hours with a bullet still in his body. She said the bullet remained lodged in his back for about 11 days. During that period, his lawyers warned that without surgery, he could face infection, permanent damage, sepsis or immobility. He then underwent surgery, she said.

His legal team described the prosecution and detention response as cruel and unsafe, citing his pain, lightheadedness and a lack of prompt medical treatment.

Ascension Seton said it followed established protocols. The hospital and ICE disputed assertions that Garces Perez had not received appropriate medical care.

Emergency physician Dr. Amy Zeidan said a retained bullet and a quick discharge do not, by themselves, establish substandard care. Assessing the treatment requires medical records, she said, and surgery near the spine can itself carry a risk of paralysis or other serious complications.

Zeidan also expressed concern about sending injured people to detention facilities because of uncertainty about the care available there. Her concern was general, rather than a finding about Garces Perez's treatment.
