Mr Justice Joel Bennathan asked 50 prospective jurors whether they had seen a 2023 television documentary or read a newspaper investigation about the case. Six who remembered the earlier coverage were dismissed.

Jury selection began on Monday at Southwark Crown Court in London in Russell Brand’s trial, in which the comedian and actor faces seven charges involving six women: three counts of rape, three counts of sexual assault and one count of indecent assault.

He also instructed prospective jurors not to research Brand or the case independently.

Charges and trial timetable

The alleged offences span 1999 to 2009 and include allegations relating to London and Bournemouth. Prosecutors have alleged that Brand raped a woman at a Labour Party conference in Bournemouth. They have also alleged that he forced a television worker into a sex act. Prosecutors have further alleged that he sexually assaulted a radio-station worker.

Brand has pleaded not guilty and remains on bail. He maintains that his sexual relationships were consensual.

Mr Justice Joel Bennathan told jurors the trial was expected to last around six weeks, the BBC reported. The Bournemouth Echo and Oxford Mail also reported that it could last up to 10 weeks.

Jury selection is expected to continue on Tuesday, with prosecutors expected to open their case on Wednesday morning.
