In a temporary restraining order, Judge Timothy J. Kelly ordered the White House to immediately restore CNN, MS NOW and Politico's press credentials for 14 days while their challenge to President Donald Trump's ban proceeds. Trump announced an immediate White House ban on the three outlets on a Friday, and the outlets said their reporters were then unable to enter White House grounds. They filed a First Amendment lawsuit the following Monday, arguing that the administration had punished them for coverage it disliked. After Kelly's ruling, CNN, MS NOW and Politico said some reporters still faced denials on Thursday, and Evan Perez said CNN correspondent Betsy Klein was still being denied access after 11 a.m. On Friday, White House officials readmitted the outlets, and regular television coverage resumed, although restrictions on some events and presidential travel continued.

The Justice Department and Trump administration said the outlets' reporting had raised national-security concerns.

Legal arguments and the case ahead

The record lacks factual support for defendants' contention that the revocation of plaintiffs' hard passes will in fact protect national security.
— Judge Timothy J. Kelly

The finding addressed the administration's stated national-security justification in the temporary order.

The department argued that White House press access is a privilege, not a constitutional right. Attorney General Todd Blanche said the White House can decide who enters the complex and which reporters travel with the president, describing those decisions as routine presidential-management choices. Responding to that position, Jameel Jaffer offered the opposing legal argument: once government officials open a space to journalists, they cannot bar a reporter because they dislike that reporter's viewpoint or work.

Trump characterised CNN, MS NOW and Politico as producers of fake, excessively negative coverage. Blanche said the president was frustrated by reporting he considered inaccurate and had a responsibility to ensure that Americans received accurate information. The outlets alleged that their credentials were revoked without notice or process because the White House objected to their reporting. They called the ban unconstitutional retaliation and warned that it would allow government officials to influence what the press reports and undermine independent journalism free of government interference.

More than 50 media organisations from across the ideological spectrum filed an amicus brief supporting the lawsuit.

Restrictions remained after credential restoration

During the ban, ABC, CBS, Fox News and NBC suspended pool coverage in solidarity with CNN or declined to replace it in the pool rotation. The action interrupted or threatened to interrupt television and visual coverage of the president and the White House.

Even after the broader readmission, some reporters were reportedly excluded from a state-dinner arrival, and CNN was not permitted to join Trump's trip to Tennessee despite the court order. Those developments left continuing uncertainty about access to particular White House events and presidential travel.

Next steps

Trump said his administration would appeal or continue challenging the ruling, while Axios reported that an appeal could be sought if the case proceeded beyond the two-week restraint period. Boutrous expects the administration to continue advancing and developing its national-security justification at the preliminary-injunction stage.

Separately, Poynter Institute speculated that Trump might abandon the confrontation after the court setback and the solidarity shown by other news organisations.

Kelly set a deadline for the outlets to seek a preliminary injunction and said he would hear further evidence.
