Sir Ranulph Fiennes is believed to have returned to an isolated Shropshire farm, the Daily Mail reports, as relatives and friends say they have been unable to contact or visit him and do not know where he is.

Cheshire Police say Fiennes is living in an appropriate setting with suitable care and professional oversight. Detective Chief Superintendent Claire Jesson said no immediate safeguarding concerns had been identified at this time.

The Independent reported that Fiennes spent seven weeks in a Welsh care home at the start of the year. Care Inspectorate Wales was reported to have found that he was unlawfully deprived of his liberty because the required authorisation did not cover his residence and restrictions.

The 82-year-old has not been seen publicly for around two years, according to BBC and Independent reports. He had previously spoken openly about a diagnosis of Parkinson's disease.

Whereabouts and restricted contact

The Daily Mail described a six-foot security gate at the Shropshire property and two modular homes newly assembled near the main residence. The newspaper argues that, if Fiennes is now at the farm, people concerned about him will have fewer external opportunities to observe his welfare.

Reports by the BBC, Oxford Mail and the Sun allege that Fiennes was moved between care homes under assumed names. They also describe restrictions on visitor access, including the use of non-disclosure agreements.

The Daily Mail also described alleged care instructions involving restrictions on socialising and the use of a disguise. An unnamed Welsh care-home owner quoted in its reporting strongly condemned the instructions, saying they would not treat an animal that way.

Alexander Millington-Cotes has described an unsuccessful attempt to find Fiennes at a care home in Cheshire. In an account reported by the Independent, he said he visited the home and staff told him Fiennes was not there.

Arabella Pepper says Fiennes's inaccessibility is worrying for friends and family. She describes relatives wanting to visit him, bring him treats and cheer him up, but being unable to do so because they do not know where he is and cannot gain access.

Anton Bowring says his priority is knowing that Fiennes is well looked after, reasonably happy and that his wishes are respected. He wants assurance that those caring for Fiennes are fulfilling the wishes Fiennes has expressed.

Reports carried by the North Wales Chronicle and TheJournal.ie say King Charles sought to contact Fiennes after Bowring raised concerns. Those reports say the attempt did not result in contact.

A power of attorney challenge

Alexander Millington-Cotes wants the lasting power of attorney removed from his mother and placed with people he believes have Fiennes's best interests in mind. He argues that Fiennes is not an isolated person and describes his present isolation as painful.

The BBC reported that Louise Millington-Cotes did not immediately respond to its requests for comment on the allegations.

Bear Grylls has called for an urgent investigation by police and the Office of the Public Guardian, saying he and others tried unsuccessfully to contact Fiennes for a year. He described reports of isolation from loved ones and care-home treatment as deeply disturbing.

Ben Fogle has also urged both bodies to investigate, saying he is deeply disturbed by reports that Fiennes has been isolated and kept from family and friends.

The Daily Mail and the Sun reported that the Office of the Public Guardian had rejected nine complaints seeking intervention. According to the Daily Mail, the body said the case was beyond its remit.

Alexander Millington-Cotes has launched a crowdfunding appeal with a £55,000 ($72,899) target. According to the Oxford Mail, donations would fund specialist legal representation and advice, court fees and efforts to establish Fiennes's exact location, as he seeks revocation of the lasting power of attorney.
