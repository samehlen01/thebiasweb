Flydubai suspends Israel flights after emergency landing in Tabuk

Flydubai flight FZ1073, operating from Dubai to Tel Aviv, was diverted for an emergency landing at Tabuk Airport in Saudi Arabia after what the airline said was an altercation in the flight deck. The airline has temporarily suspended flights to and from Israel while authorities investigate the incident.

Flight-tracking data from Flightradar24 recorded FZ1073 transmitting an emergency signal followed by an unlawful-interference signal; it also showed the aircraft descending from about 34,000 feet to 17,000 feet in less than two minutes. The recorded signals and descent did not establish the cause of the incident.

After the landing, Saudi airport authorities said both cockpit crew members were injured and received hospital treatment. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the alleged attacking pilot was arrested after the landing and questioned by Saudi authorities. Later that day, on September 30, passengers from FZ1073 were transported from Tabuk to Tel Aviv on replacement Flydubai aircraft, the airline said.

Accounts of the cockpit struggle

Flydubai said the cause and motive of the incident remained unknown and under formal investigation, and it did not confirm the reported stabbing. The airline urged the public not to draw conclusions before authorities completed their investigation. Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz characterized the incident as an attempted jihadist terrorist attack, based on preliminary accounts that said passengers overpowered the alleged attacking pilot and restored control to other crew members. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said one of the pilots stabbed the other and apparently attempted to crash the aircraft.

The accounts agree that the aircraft was secured but differ over who intervened: Flydubai said its on-duty crew secured the aircraft, while passenger accounts and Israeli officials described passengers intervening in the cockpit.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu identified the injured captain as Smit Machchhar, an Indian national. India’s embassy in Riyadh said Machchhar was hospitalized in Tabuk and reported stable. Netanyahu said Machchhar fought back while seriously injured and opened the cockpit door, allowing passengers and crew members to overpower the alleged attacking pilot. Netanyahu praised Captain Smit Machchhar, passengers and crew members who intervened as heroes who prevented a disaster.

An unnamed Israeli security official, cited by Axios and CBS News, said Israeli authorities scrambled fighter jets and temporarily closed Israeli airspace after the emergency signals. The official said authorities feared a possible hijacking or attack. Netanyahu instructed Israel’s security establishment to prepare for potential additional threats.

In a separate CBS News report, an Israeli security official said authorities were examining whether the episode was an attempted terrorist attack and whether the alleged attacker acted alone.

Investigation

Separately, The Guardian cited Stanislav Bukhman, who said he expected investigators to examine pilot vetting, crew screening and how the flight deck was accessed.

The UAE General Civil Aviation Authority said it was investigating the incident, including its operational and security aspects.
